AI Got Good at Feelings. Now What?

by divya

For most of the last decade the argument against these systems was that they could not do warmth. They could summarise, they could retrieve, they could rearrange — but the moment a person needed to feel heard, the machinery showed through. That objection has quietly stopped being true, and almost nobody has updated what they build because of it.

The interesting question was never whether a model can produce a sentence that lands as kind. It plainly can. The question is what happens to the people on the other side of it once they know that it can, and what a designer owes them at that moment.

We are very good at designing for capability and very bad at designing for the disclosure of capability. A product that can console you is a different object from one that merely answers you, and pretending otherwise is a decision, not a default.

The honest version of this product tells you what it is before it tells you anything else. The dishonest version is warmer, tests better, and leaves the person worse off in a way no metric on the dashboard will ever surface.
